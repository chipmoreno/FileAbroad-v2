## What Is Form 2555?

**Form 2555, Foreign Earned Income Exclusion**, is the form you file with your annual federal tax return to claim the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) under Internal Revenue Code Section 911. The FEIE allows qualifying US citizens and resident aliens living abroad to exclude a significant portion of their foreign earned income from US federal income tax.

For tax year 2025, the exclusion amount is **$130,000**. For tax year 2026, it rises to **$132,900**.

Form 2555 is also used to claim the **Foreign Housing Exclusion** or **Foreign Housing Deduction**, which can further reduce your taxable income by excluding or deducting qualifying housing expenses above a base amount.

## Who Qualifies to File Form 2555

To claim the FEIE, you must satisfy **three requirements**:

1. **Your tax home must be in a foreign country.** Your tax home is generally your regular place of business or, if you have none, your regular place of abode.
2. **You must have foreign earned income.** The income must be from services performed in a foreign country.
3. **You must meet either the Physical Presence Test or the Bona Fide Residence Test.**

### The Physical Presence Test

You must be physically present in a foreign country or countries for at least **330 full days** during any 12-month period. Key rules:

- The 12-month period does not need to align with the calendar year.
- A "full day" is midnight-to-midnight. The day you depart the US does not count as a foreign day.
- Days in the US count against you. You can spend a maximum of 35 days in the US during your 12-month period.
- Days in international waters or airspace do not count toward either total.

### The Bona Fide Residence Test

You must be a **bona fide resident** of a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes an entire tax year (January 1 through December 31). Key rules:

- Brief trips to the US do not automatically disqualify you.
- The IRS considers intent, visa status, local tax registration, housing, and community ties.
- You cannot claim bona fide residence in a country where you are present illegally.

## Parts of Form 2555

### Part I — General Information

Basic information about you, your employer, your foreign address, and the nature of your work.

### Part II — Bona Fide Residence Test

Complete this section **only** if you are claiming the FEIE under the Bona Fide Residence Test. You must list the foreign country of residence, describe your visa status, and provide information about your intent to remain abroad.

### Part III — Physical Presence Test

Complete this section **only** if you are claiming the FEIE under the Physical Presence Test. You must list your travel dates showing all arrivals and departures from foreign countries during your qualifying 12-month period.

**Critical:** Keep detailed travel records. The IRS can request documentation to substantiate your claim.

### Part IV — Foreign Earned Income

List all your foreign earned income by source (wages, self-employment, professional fees, etc.). Exclude investment income, pension income, and US-source income.

### Part V — Housing Exclusion or Deduction

If you are claiming the Foreign Housing Exclusion (as an employee) or Foreign Housing Deduction (as a self-employed person), complete this section.

For 2025:
- **Base housing amount:** $20,800 (16% of $130,000)
- **General cap:** $39,000 (30% of $130,000)
- **Location-specific caps:** The IRS publishes higher limits for expensive cities (e.g., London, Hong Kong, Tokyo, Singapore, Geneva).

Qualifying expenses include rent, utilities (except telephone), insurance, occupancy taxes, and parking. Mortgage payments and furniture do not qualify.

### Part VI — Final Computation

This section calculates your total exclusion (FEIE + housing) and determines how much of your foreign earned income is excluded from tax.

## Common Mistakes on Form 2555

### Mistake 1: Claiming the FEIE on Non-Earned Income

The FEIE applies **only** to earned income (wages, salaries, self-employment). Dividends, interest, capital gains, and rental income do not qualify. These must be reported and taxed normally.

### Mistake 2: Failing the Physical Presence Test by One Day

The 330-day requirement is strict. If you spend 36 days in the US, you fail the test. Track all travel carefully, including layovers that count as US days.

### Mistake 3: Not Prorating for Partial-Year Qualification

If you only qualify for part of the year, you must prorate the exclusion. Do not claim the full $130,000 if you only had 200 qualifying days.

### Mistake 4: Confusing Tax Home with Domicile

Your tax home is where you work or live regularly. Your domicile is your permanent legal home. You can have a foreign tax home while maintaining a US domicile (which affects state tax obligations).

### Mistake 5: Forgetting the Five-Year Lock

If you revoke the FEIE to switch to the Foreign Tax Credit, you cannot re-elect the FEIE for five years. Make sure the switch is worth the long-term commitment.

## Evidence to Keep for Form 2555

The strongest FEIE file connects the income to the place where the services were performed and connects the qualifying days to reliable travel records. Keep passport stamps, immigration records, flight confirmations, border histories, leases, utility records, work calendars, employer letters, contracts, invoices, payroll slips, and foreign tax filings. No single document proves every requirement. The file should explain the tax home, the work location, the qualifying test, the income received, and any housing costs claimed.

### Remote workers and employer changes

A remote worker can have foreign earned income while serving a US employer, but the analysis still depends on where the services were performed and whether the tax home is abroad. Track moves between countries, temporary US workdays, employer changes, bonuses, equity compensation, and periods of leave. A payroll address or bank account does not by itself establish the place of performance. If the work arrangement changed during the year, split the income and days by period.

### Housing and partial-year calculations

Housing calculations require receipts and a clear allocation between qualifying expenses, employer reimbursements, personal expenses, and shared costs. Apply the statutory base amount and location cap for the correct tax year rather than carrying forward a prior-year worksheet. For a partial year, prorate where the instructions require it and retain the move-in, move-out, and qualifying-day dates. A housing calculation can be affected by employer-provided lodging, family members, roommates, and time spent in a temporary location.

## FEIE and Self-Employment Tax

The FEIE excludes income from **income tax** but does **not** reduce your **self-employment tax** (Social Security and Medicare). If you are self-employed abroad:

- You still owe SE tax on your net self-employment income.
- A **Totalization Agreement** may exempt you from US SE tax if you are paying into a foreign social security system.
- You cannot deduct the employer portion of SE tax and also claim the FEIE on the same income.

## When NOT to Use Form 2555

The FEIE is not always the best choice. Consider the **Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116)** instead if:

- You live in a high-tax country (e.g., Germany, France, UK, Netherlands).
- Your foreign taxes paid exceed your US tax liability.
- You earn significantly more than the exclusion amount (stacking effect pushes higher income into higher brackets).
- You want to preserve your ability to contribute to an IRA (excluded income may not count as compensation).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who can file Form 2555?

You can file Form 2555 if you are a US citizen or resident alien, your tax home is in a foreign country, and you meet either the Physical Presence Test (330 full days in foreign countries in a 12-month period) or the Bona Fide Residence Test (resident of a foreign country for an uninterrupted period including a full calendar year).

### What is the maximum FEIE amount for 2025?

For tax year 2025, the maximum Foreign Earned Income Exclusion is $130,000 per qualifying individual. For tax year 2026, the exclusion rises to $132,900 (Rev. Proc. 2025-32). If both spouses qualify, each can exclude up to the maximum, for a combined household exclusion of $260,000 (2025) or $265,800 (2026).

### Can I claim the FEIE for only part of the year?

Yes. If you qualify for only part of the tax year, your exclusion is prorated on a daily basis. For 2025, divide $130,000 by 365 days to get approximately $356.16 per qualifying day. You must still meet a qualifying test for the portion of the year you claim.

### What income qualifies for the FEIE?

Only foreign earned income qualifies. This includes wages, salaries, professional fees, bonuses, commissions, and self-employment income from services performed in a foreign country. It does not include investment income (dividends, interest, capital gains), pension income, or income earned in the United States.

### What happens if I revoke my FEIE election?

If you revoke your FEIE election, you generally cannot re-elect it for five tax years without IRS approval. This makes the decision to switch from FEIE to the Foreign Tax Credit (or vice versa) a long-term commitment that should be evaluated carefully.
