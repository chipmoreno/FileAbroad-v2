# Self-Employment Tax for Americans Abroad

Working for yourself from another country creates several separate U.S. questions. You may have business income, deductible expenses, self-employment tax, foreign income tax, information returns, state-tax exposure, and a local-country business registration. The correct order is to map the activity and entity before choosing a form.

## Start with the person and the activity

Describe what you do, who contracts with you, where the services are performed, how customers pay, and whether anyone else owns the business. A freelancer providing services personally is not analyzed the same way as a foreign corporation, partnership, or single-member entity.

Collect:

- Contracts, invoices, payment-platform statements, and expenses.
- Dates and countries where services were performed.
- Business registration, ownership, and operating agreements.
- Foreign tax filings and social-insurance records.
- Prior U.S. returns, entity elections, and information returns.

## Schedule C and business expenses

Many sole proprietors report gross receipts and ordinary, necessary business expenses on Schedule C. Keep a reconciliation from the business bank or payment platform to the U.S. return. Expenses need a business purpose and may require allocation when they also serve personal use.

Foreign-currency records are part of the tax file. Record the date, local amount, exchange-rate method, and U.S.-dollar result. Do not rely on a year-end account balance to reconstruct an entire year of receipts.

## Self-employment tax is separate from income tax

The FEIE and Foreign Tax Credit address income-tax calculations; they do not automatically remove self-employment tax. Review net earnings from self-employment, the applicable Social Security and Medicare rules, and whether a totalization agreement assigns coverage to the foreign country. A certificate of coverage or equivalent evidence may be necessary.

## FEIE, FTC, or both

If the services produce foreign earned income, Form 2555 may be relevant when the physical-presence or bona-fide-residence test is met. Foreign income taxes may support Form 1116 if the tax and source rules are satisfied. The same income cannot be excluded and also support a credit for the related tax. Model the options over more than one year when income is variable.

## Foreign entities and Form 5471/8865/8858

A foreign company may be a corporation, partnership, or disregarded entity for U.S. purposes. The classification can trigger Form 5471, Form 8865, Form 8858, GILTI, Subpart F, or other reporting. Do not assume a local LLC is the U.S. equivalent of a U.S. LLC.

## Quarterly estimates and state domicile

Clients without U.S. payroll withholding may need quarterly estimates. The calculation should include self-employment tax, foreign credits, the FEIE, and prior-year safe-harbor rules. Separately, a former state may continue to assert domicile or statutory residency until ties are severed and documented.
