# Green Card Holders Abroad: U.S. Tax Filing and Residency Guide

Moving abroad does not automatically end the U.S. tax obligations of a lawful permanent resident. A green card holder may continue to be a U.S. resident for federal tax purposes, report worldwide income, and file foreign-account forms while living overseas. A card’s expiration, a long absence, or a plan to abandon status is not a complete tax conclusion.

## Keep immigration and tax timelines separate

Build a timeline of:

- Green card issuance and renewal.
- Dates outside the United States.
- Any immigration order, I-407 filing, or final abandonment action.
- Treaty residence and foreign tax residency.
- U.S. returns and international forms filed for each year.

The date a document expired may not be the date lawful permanent resident status ended. The expatriation rules can use a formal termination date and can look back at prior years.

## Worldwide income and foreign accounts

While treated as a U.S. resident for tax purposes, a green card holder generally reviews worldwide income: wages, business income, interest, dividends, pensions, rental income, and gains. Foreign accounts and assets can also trigger FBAR, Form 8938, Form 3520, Form 5471, Form 8621, or other information returns.

Do not wait until the day of abandonment to reconstruct five or six years of records. Keep account statements, tax returns, entity documents, pension statements, and proof of foreign taxes in an organized file.

## FEIE, FTC, and treaty analysis

A resident abroad may be eligible for the FEIE or Foreign Tax Credit, but the ordinary requirements still apply. A treaty can affect residence or source, but the saving clause and the green card holder’s status must be reviewed. A treaty position should be documented with the relevant article and Form 8833 analysis where required.

## Abandonment and Form 8854

Ending lawful permanent residence can create an expatriation-tax review for a long-term resident. The review may include the number of prior years as a resident, average tax liability, net worth, tax compliance certification, deferred compensation, and deemed-disposition rules. Form 8854 is not a substitute for the underlying return and compliance work.

## State tax and domicile

A former U.S. state may assert residency even when federal immigration status has changed. Review home ownership, voter registration, driver license, family location, business activity, and days in the state. Keep evidence of the new domicile and the date ties were severed.
