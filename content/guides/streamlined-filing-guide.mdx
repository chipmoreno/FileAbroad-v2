The Streamlined Filing Compliance Procedures can provide a defined route for certain taxpayers to submit delinquent or amended returns and resolve related tax and penalty obligations. For Americans living abroad, the relevant route is usually the **Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures (SFOP)**.

This is not a one-size-fits-all package. The filing years, required information returns, prior IRS activity, residency facts, and the taxpayer's own non-willfulness certification all matter. The [current IRS Streamlined overview](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) and [current SFOP instructions](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-taxpayers-residing-outside-the-united-states) control if this guide and an IRS page ever differ.

## What the Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures Do

SFOP is for eligible individual taxpayers, including estates of individual taxpayers, whose covered failures resulted from non-willful conduct and who meet the applicable non-residency requirement. It is not an amnesty, closing agreement, or promise that the IRS will never review the returns.

When an eligible taxpayer follows all current instructions, the IRS provides the penalty treatment described for SFOP. The taxpayer still submits complete and accurate returns, files required FBARs and information returns, pays tax and interest, and signs Form 14653 under penalties of perjury.

The domestic Streamlined procedure has different residency and penalty rules. This guide focuses on the foreign procedure for taxpayers residing outside the United States.

## Who May Qualify for SFOP

The IRS rules contain both general Streamlined requirements and SFOP-specific requirements. A paid consultation is used to collect the facts needed to define FileAbroad's preparation scope; it is not a legal opinion about eligibility or willfulness.

### Non-willful conduct

The taxpayer must certify that failures to report income, pay tax, and submit required information returns, including FBARs, resulted from non-willful conduct. The IRS defines non-willful conduct as conduct due to negligence, inadvertence, mistake, or a good-faith misunderstanding of the requirements of the law.

The certification belongs to the taxpayer. FileAbroad can organize a clear factual chronology from information the client provides, but does not decide whether conduct was willful or provide a legal opinion about the certification.

### Non-residency

For a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident, the IRS test looks to at least one of the most recent three years for which the return due date, including a properly applied-for extension, has passed. In that year, the person must have had no U.S. abode and must have been physically outside the United States for at least 330 full days. For a joint submission, both spouses must satisfy the applicable test.

For an individual who is not a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident, the IRS uses a different test based on whether the person failed to meet the substantial presence test in at least one of the relevant three years.

Travel days alone do not answer every residency question because the U.S.-abode requirement can also matter.

### No current civil examination or criminal investigation

The IRS says the Streamlined procedures are unavailable when it has initiated a civil examination of the taxpayer's returns for any tax year or when the taxpayer is under criminal investigation. That is narrower than saying every IRS letter or communication automatically disqualifies someone.

All IRS notices, examinations, investigations, penalty assessments, and prior delinquent or amended filings should still be disclosed during scope screening because they may affect the correct path and the work FileAbroad can accept.

### Valid taxpayer identification number

Streamlined submissions require a valid taxpayer identification number. The IRS instructions describe a possible submission process for certain people eligible for an ITIN who include a complete ITIN application.

## What Goes Into an SFOP Submission

The exact calendar years move with filing deadlines, so a reliable guide should not hard-code them.

### Three return years

The current SFOP instructions call for complete and accurate delinquent Forms 1040 or amended Forms 1040-X for each of the most recent three years whose return due dates, including properly applied-for extensions, have passed.

Required information returns must accompany those returns. Depending on the facts, that can include forms such as 8938, 3520, or 5471. Those forms are not automatically included in every FileAbroad quote; the written scope identifies which forms the firm has accepted and prices any additional work.

Specified foreign corporation and section 965 facts can require certain 2017, 2018, or later returns outside the standard three-year period. The IRS publishes [separate Streamlined section 965 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures-and-section-965), so "three years" should not be treated as a universal cap before the facts are screened.

### Six FBAR years

The current instructions call for delinquent FBARs for each of the most recent six years whose FBAR due dates have passed. FBARs are filed electronically through the FinCEN BSA E-Filing System with the explanation required by the Streamlined instructions.

When FileAbroad is authorized to file an FBAR on a client's behalf, the client signs FinCEN Form 114a and FileAbroad retains that authorization; Form 114a is not submitted with the FBAR.

### Form 14653

Form 14653 is the taxpayer's certification that the person is eligible for SFOP, has filed all required FBARs, and that the covered failures resulted from non-willful conduct. The taxpayer signs the certification under penalties of perjury.

FileAbroad's role is factual preparation: organize the client's chronology, identify missing facts, place the client's statements into a coherent draft, and assemble the copies required by current instructions. The client must verify every fact and make the certification. If the client is uncertain about intent or wants a legal opinion, that question should be resolved with qualified legal counsel before signing.

### Tax, interest, and submission method

The taxpayer must submit the tax and applicable statutory interest due. The federal returns, information returns, certification, and payment materials are sent in paper form to the current IRS address in the SFOP instructions; delinquent FBARs are filed electronically.

Always verify the current official address and filing instructions before sending a package because agency procedures can change.

## SFOP Penalty Treatment

For an eligible taxpayer who complies with all current SFOP instructions, the IRS says the taxpayer will not be subject to failure-to-file, failure-to-pay, accuracy-related, information-return, or FBAR penalties for the covered submission.

That favorable treatment has important limits:

- Tax and statutory interest remain due.
- Previously assessed penalties for the relevant years are not automatically abated.
- The IRS can select the returns for examination under its regular processes.
- If an examination finds fraud, a willful FBAR violation, or an additional deficiency, different penalties or consequences may apply.
- The submission does not end with a closing agreement or an IRS acceptance letter.

This is why FileAbroad describes **specified penalty relief for a qualifying, correctly completed submission**, not a blanket promise of "no penalties."
