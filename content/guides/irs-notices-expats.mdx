# IRS Notices and Audits for Americans Abroad

An IRS notice is a workflow trigger, not a conclusion. Expats often face added complexity because records are overseas, foreign institutions use different tax years, and a notice can involve Form 8938, FBAR data, pension reporting, or a third-party information mismatch. Preserve the notice and start with the response date.

## First-response checklist

1. Scan every page and preserve the envelope.
2. Identify the tax year, notice number, response deadline, and IRS address.
3. Pull the filed return, account transcript, wage and income transcript, and payment record.
4. Locate the supporting bank, brokerage, pension, entity, and foreign-tax records.
5. Decide whether the response requires a correction, explanation, payment, or representation.
6. Send the response using a trackable method and retain proof of delivery.

Do not rely on a summary in an email or a screenshot of the first page. The notice’s attachments and response instructions can change the required action.

## CP2000 and information mismatches

A CP2000 is generally based on information reported to the IRS that does not match the return. For an expat, the mismatch may involve U.S. brokerage income, retirement distributions, interest, foreign-source income entered on the wrong line, or a duplicate form. Reconcile payer records, basis, dates, currency, and the filed return before agreeing or disagreeing.

## Foreign-account and international notices

An IRS notice can concern Form 8938, Form 3520, Form 5471, Form 8621, or another information return. An FBAR issue may involve FinCEN rather than the IRS. A response should identify the agency, form, years, and requested records. Filing one missing form may not resolve the entire notice.

## Statute and record retention

International information-return failures can affect the ordinary assessment timeline. Do not assume that a return is closed simply because three years have passed. Preserve the returns, filed forms, delivery evidence, statements, and correspondence for the relevant period.

## When to escalate

Obtain representation if the notice alleges fraud, negligence, substantial understatement, unreported foreign accounts, willful conduct, or multiple years of missing information. Do not send a casual narrative that admits facts or signs a certification before the response strategy is reviewed.
