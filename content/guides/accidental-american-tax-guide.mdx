# Accidental American Taxes

Many people discover U.S. citizenship through a bank request, a passport application, an inheritance, or a conversation with a parent. The discovery can feel like a tax emergency, but the first step is fact gathering. Citizenship, tax residence, filing history, account reporting, and renunciation are separate questions.

## Confirm the citizenship facts

Gather birth certificates, parent citizenship records, passports, prior consular records, and any correspondence from a bank or government. The [IRS guidance on U.S. citizenship by birth or through a U.S. citizen parent](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-by-birth-or-through-a-us-citizen-parent) is a tax starting point, not a replacement for a citizenship determination by the appropriate authority.

## Identify the filing and reporting exposure

U.S. citizens generally file based on worldwide income and applicable filing thresholds even when they live abroad. The federal return may be accompanied by FBAR, Form 8938, Form 3520, Form 8621, Form 5471, or other information returns depending on the accounts, investments, gifts, trusts, and entities involved.

The [IRS filing requirements for citizens abroad](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-and-residents-abroad-filing-requirements) and [FinCEN FBAR guidance](https://www.fincen.gov/purpose-fbar) should be reviewed before estimating the number of missing years. Do not assume that no U.S. income means no U.S. filing or reporting obligation.

## Evaluate the right compliance path

Potential paths can include filing current returns, filing delinquent returns and information forms, Streamlined Foreign Offshore, a different IRS procedure, or a referral for legal representation. The correct route depends on residence, years missed, prior IRS contact, knowledge of the obligation, accounts, income, and whether the conduct was non-willful.

FileAbroad’s [Streamlined Filing guide](/guides/streamlined-filing-guide) and [Form 14653 page](/forms/14653-streamlined) explain the educational framework. A non-willfulness certification is a factual statement with consequences; FileAbroad does not make a willfulness determination or promise penalty-free treatment.

## Renunciation is not a shortcut

Renunciation is a legal and administrative decision, not a substitute for understanding past filing obligations. The [IRS expatriation-tax guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/expatriation-tax) and [Form 8854 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8854) explain why five-year compliance certification, net worth, tax-liability, and other facts may matter. Citizenship and immigration advice belongs with the appropriate legal or consular authority.

## Organize before contacting a preparer

Prepare a timeline of residence, citizenship discovery, income, accounts, gifts, investments, business ownership, prior returns, IRS letters, and any passport or bank deadlines. Include maximum account balances and the countries where accounts were held. Keep sensitive documents for a secure upload process after an engagement is accepted.

For a preliminary scope review, use the [Accidental Americans page](/personas/accidental-americans) and book a [consultation](/consultation). State the years unfiled and broad account or income categories; do not send SSNs, account numbers, passports, or tax documents through the public form or WhatsApp.
