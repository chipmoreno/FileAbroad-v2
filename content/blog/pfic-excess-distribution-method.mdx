# PFIC Excess Distribution Method: Section 1291 Explained

The PFIC rules can produce unexpected tax when a U.S. person holds a foreign mutual fund, ETF, unit trust, or other foreign pooled investment. If no qualifying election applies, the holding may be treated as a **section 1291 fund**, and an excess distribution calculation can allocate tax across the holding period.

## What counts as an excess distribution?

The current Form 8621 instructions generally define an excess distribution as the part of a distribution that is greater than 125% of the average distributions received during the prior three years, or the shorter period the shareholder held the stock. The first-year distribution is not treated as an excess distribution under the same averaging rule.

The entire gain from disposing of section 1291 fund stock is generally treated as an excess distribution. This means a sale can trigger a calculation even when the investor never received a large cash distribution.

## Allocation across the holding period

The excess distribution is allocated on a per-day basis over the shareholder’s holding period. The current-year portion and any pre-PFIC portion are treated differently from portions allocated to prior PFIC years. The prior-year PFIC portions can be subject to a separate tax and interest charge rather than ordinary current-year tax alone.

The calculation requires records that many investors do not keep in a tax-ready format:

- Acquisition and disposition dates.
- Share counts and basis by lot.
- Every distribution and reinvestment.
- Prior-year PFIC classifications and Form 8621 filings.
- Currency conversions and foreign account statements.
- Election statements, annual information statements, and marketability evidence.

## Why the holding period matters

Two investors who sell the same fund for the same dollar gain can have different results if one held it for two years and the other held it for ten. A longer holding period can spread the allocation across more prior PFIC years and increase the record-reconstruction burden.

Do not assume that a fund’s current label answers the historical question. A product can change structure, domicile, share class, or reporting status. Obtain the issuer’s legal identity and review the period actually held.

## Section 1291 is not a universal “penalty” label

The section 1291 calculation is a statutory tax method, not a discretionary penalty. It can still be expensive and difficult to model, especially when prior-year U.S. tax rates, interest, distributions, and currency values are involved. A taxpayer should not sell a foreign fund solely because a generic article says it is a PFIC, and should not keep it solely because an advisor says the tax is “only on sale.”

## Comparing the main PFIC paths

- **QEF election:** Generally requires an annual information statement from the fund and current-year inclusions based on the fund’s ordinary earnings and net capital gain.
- **Mark-to-market election:** Generally requires marketable stock under the applicable rules and has its own inclusion and loss limits.
- **Section 1291 method:** Can apply when neither election is available or has been made, with the excess-distribution allocation and interest calculation.

The best path depends on the fund, election timing, tax year, basis, liquidity, and the taxpayer’s larger portfolio. It is not a one-size-fits-all investment recommendation.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.
