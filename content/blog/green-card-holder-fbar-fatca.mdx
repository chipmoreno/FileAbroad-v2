# Green Card Holders Abroad: FBAR and FATCA Reporting

Living abroad does not remove a lawful permanent resident from a foreign-account review. The FBAR and Form 8938 use different rules, so build one asset inventory and test it against both forms.

## Account inventory

List bank, brokerage, pension, insurance, business, trust, and joint accounts. Record country, legal owner, signature authority, maximum value, year-end value, and currency. Keep statements for accounts opened or closed during the year.

## Separate FBAR from Form 8938

The FBAR is filed electronically with FinCEN and generally focuses on foreign financial accounts. Form 8938 is attached to the IRS return and can reach specified foreign financial assets beyond accounts. The same asset can be reportable on both.

Start with a complete fact inventory: filing year, U.S. status, residence and work locations, legal owner, dates, currencies, related entities, and prior filings. ## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.
