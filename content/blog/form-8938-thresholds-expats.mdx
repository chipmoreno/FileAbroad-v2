# Form 8938 Thresholds for Americans Living Abroad

Form 8938 is the FATCA information return for specified foreign financial assets. It is not a second FBAR and it is not a tax calculation by itself. It is an attachment to an income-tax return when the taxpayer meets the filing and asset-value rules for the year.

## The threshold is only the first question

The threshold analysis has several moving parts:

Start with the [FATCA guide](/guides/fatca-guide), then compare the result against the [FBAR guide](/guides/fbar-guide) because the forms use different definitions and thresholds. The [FATCA compliance service](/services/fatca-compliance) can organize the asset inventory and supporting valuation records within a written scope.

1. **Are you required to file a federal income-tax return?** Form 8938 generally travels with that return; a person who is not required to file an income-tax return generally is not required to file Form 8938 solely because they own foreign assets.
2. **Are you a specified individual or specified domestic entity?** The applicable rules differ.
3. **Do your specified foreign financial assets exceed the applicable year-end or anytime threshold?** Filing status and residence affect the threshold.
4. **What assets and interests are included?** A foreign bank or brokerage account is only one category. Foreign securities, interests in foreign entities, and certain foreign pensions or deferred-compensation interests may also be relevant.

The IRS instructions are the controlling source for the filing year. A number copied from an older article can be wrong even when the general framework is right.

## What counts as a specified foreign financial asset?

The IRS describes specified foreign financial assets broadly. Common examples include:

- Bank, deposit, and brokerage accounts maintained by a foreign financial institution.
- Foreign stocks, securities, and interests in foreign entities held outside a reportable financial account.
- Certain interests in foreign estates, foreign pension plans, and deferred-compensation plans.
- Financial instruments or contracts held for investment with a foreign issuer or counterparty.

Directly owned foreign real estate is not automatically a specified foreign financial asset. A foreign company, partnership, trust, or account that owns the real estate can change the analysis. The legal owner and the ownership chain matter more than the label used by a bank or investment platform.

## Valuation and currency conversion

The form uses fair-market-value concepts and requires foreign-currency values to be converted into U.S. dollars under the applicable instructions. Keep statements showing year-end values, maximum or periodic values where relevant, exchange-rate support, and ownership percentages. If an account statement is not in dollars, retain the method used to convert it.

Joint ownership also matters. The treatment can differ depending on whether the joint owner is a spouse who files jointly, a spouse who files separately, or another person. Do not automatically divide every joint account in half.

## Form 8938 and the FBAR can overlap

An account may be reported on both forms because the forms serve different statutes and agencies. The FATCA form has duplicative-reporting exceptions for certain assets reported on other forms, but those exceptions do not mean the asset disappears from the overall analysis. Read the current Part IV instructions and identify the other form before relying on an exception.

The forms also ask different questions. The FBAR focuses on foreign financial accounts and financial interest or signature authority. Form 8938 can reach specified foreign financial assets that are not traditional bank accounts. A complete compliance review should compare the asset inventory against both sets of instructions.

## A practical document checklist

Before preparing Form 8938, collect:

- The filing status and whether the foreign-residence test for the applicable threshold is satisfied.
- Account statements and maximum-value records for each foreign institution.
- Issuer names, legal domiciles, and ownership percentages for foreign securities and entities.
- Foreign pension, deferred-compensation, estate, and trust documents.
- Currency-conversion records and the valuation date used.
- Copies of FBARs, Forms 3520, Forms 5471, Forms 8621, or other forms that may affect duplicative reporting.

If a provider calls an account “tax sheltered,” “retirement,” or “insurance,” treat that as a product description—not a U.S. reporting conclusion.

## Scope boundary

This article explains the structure of the Form 8938 review. It does not determine whether a particular asset is reportable, whether an exception applies, or whether a foreign entity is classified in a particular way. Those conclusions require the current instructions and the facts of the asset, owner, institution, and tax year.
