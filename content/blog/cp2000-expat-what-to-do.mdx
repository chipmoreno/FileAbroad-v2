Receiving an IRS CP2000 notice while living abroad can be unsettling. The notice usually means the IRS received information from a payer or other third party that does not match something reported on your return. It does not, by itself, prove that you intentionally omitted income or that the IRS has issued a final assessment.

The notice is a proposed adjustment. The exact response date, address, upload option, and supporting documents are controlled by the notice you received. Do not rely on a generic deadline from an article; use the instructions on your own letter. The IRS describes the CP2000 series and response process in its [current CP2000 notice guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/understanding-your-cp2000-series-notice).

## Start with a notice-to-return comparison

Create a simple reconciliation before deciding what to send:

## Related records to reconcile

Use the [expat tax guide](/guides/expat-tax-guide) to check the federal return, and compare the notice to the [Foreign Tax Credit guide](/guides/foreign-tax-credit-guide) when the mismatch involves foreign withholding, Form 1116, or exchange rates. If the comparison needs return reconstruction, the [expat tax filing service](/services/expat-tax-filing) can organize the notice, transcript, and source records within a written scope.

1. Identify each income or payment item listed by the IRS.
2. Find the corresponding Form W-2, 1099, brokerage statement, pension statement, or payer record.
3. Compare the amount, tax year, taxpayer identification number, and whether the item belongs to you or a spouse.
4. Compare the item with the filed Form 1040 and schedules.
5. Record whether the issue is omitted income, duplicate reporting, wrong tax year, incorrect payer information, or a tax form that was filed but not matched.

Americans abroad should also check whether the mismatch is connected to a foreign-currency conversion, a foreign pension distribution, a brokerage statement, or an information return that was filed separately from the income tax return. A foreign account report such as the FBAR does not replace reporting taxable income on Form 1040, and filing Form 8938 does not automatically resolve an income mismatch.

## If the IRS information is correct

If the proposed change is accurate, follow the notice instructions and calculate the resulting tax, interest, and any payment amount. Review the effect on related items before accepting the proposed change. For example, additional foreign income may change a foreign tax credit limitation, a self-employment calculation, or the treatment of an exclusion.

The IRS says taxpayers should respond as directed by the notice and keep copies of all correspondence. Do not send original records. If you need more time, follow the notice’s procedure rather than assuming an extension is automatic.

## If the IRS information is wrong or incomplete

Respond with a clear explanation and records that address each proposed adjustment. Common examples include:

- income reported under the wrong Social Security number;
- a duplicate Form 1099 or corrected payer statement;
- a payment belonging to a different tax year;
- income already included on the return under a different form or name;
- foreign tax, basis, or currency-conversion records that change the calculation.

If the return itself needs correction, the notice may direct you to file an amended return. Do not file an amended return merely because you received a notice; first follow the notice’s instructions and make sure the amended figures agree with the response explanation.

## Expat-specific review points

Before responding, check whether the proposed change affects Form 2555, Form 1116, Form 8938, Schedule B, Schedule C, Schedule E, pension reporting, or state filings. A change to one line can affect several connected forms. If the notice involves foreign entities, trusts, PFICs, or a prior-year compliance program, the matter may require attorney or specialist review.

FileAbroad can screen an accepted preparation scope, organize records, and prepare a response when the matter fits the service boundaries. Legal representation, willfulness questions, appeals, and disputes requiring tax counsel should be referred.

For a notice review, [start the intake](/intake) with the tax year, notice type, response date, and a short description. Do not send Social Security numbers, account numbers, passports, or tax documents through an unprotected preliminary message.
