# FATCA Penalties for Form 8938 Noncompliance

Form 8938 is an information return attached to a federal income-tax return. It is not the same filing as the FBAR, and the consequences of missing it should be analyzed separately. The right response to a missed form begins with reconstructing the filing obligation—not with copying a penalty number from an old article.

## The penalty framework has multiple stages

The current Form 8938 instructions describe an initial failure-to-file penalty and additional consequences when a taxpayer continues to fail to provide the information after IRS notice. Related tax underpayments can create separate accuracy-related or other penalties. The actual exposure depends on the year, the required form, the notice history, the assets, and the tax return.

The FBAR has its own civil-penalty framework. Filing an FBAR does not cure a missing Form 8938, and filing Form 8938 does not cure a missing FBAR. Review both requirements independently.

## Reconstruct the missing-year facts

For each year, collect:

- The filed Form 1040 and all schedules.
- Filing status and residence facts.
- Foreign account and asset statements.
- Maximum and year-end values with currency support.
- Forms 3520, 5471, 8621, 8854, or other connected information returns.
- Prior IRS notices and correspondence.
- Evidence of reliance, misunderstanding, illness, or other facts relevant to reasonable cause.

Do not estimate one year from another without checking ownership, account balances, and tax-year rules. A foreign fund, pension, trust, or company can change the form analysis even when the balance is modest.

## Information reporting and tax understatement are different issues

Form 8938 generally reports assets; it does not replace reporting the income those assets generated. A missed information return and an omitted interest payment are separate problems. Correcting the form without reviewing the underlying income can leave the return inconsistent. Conversely, an asset may be reportable even when it produced no income.

The statute of limitations and assessment rules can also be affected by missing international information. The current instructions and the facts of the year control; do not assume that the ordinary three-year timeline answers every question.

## Reasonable cause is fact-specific

Reasonable-cause arguments are not a generic “I did not know” paragraph. They should explain what happened, what the taxpayer knew or reasonably believed, what records were available, when the issue was discovered, and what corrective steps were taken. Preserve contemporaneous evidence and avoid making a statement that overstates the facts.

If the facts suggest intentional concealment, false statements, or a broader offshore-compliance issue, obtain attorney advice before submitting a narrative. A preparer should not make a willfulness conclusion outside their scope.

## A safer correction workflow

1. Determine whether Form 8938 was required for each year.
2. Rebuild the asset inventory and values.
3. Reconcile income and other international forms.
4. Identify notices, deadlines, and any pending examination.
5. Select the correction procedure appropriate to the facts.
6. Prepare the form and any explanation consistently with the underlying return.
7. Keep proof of filing, delivery, and the completed workpapers.

## Classify the asset before testing the threshold

For a FATCA question, build the inventory by legal owner and product structure. Record whether the item is a deposit account, securities account, non-account investment, insurance or annuity product, pension interest, entity interest, trust interest, or digital-asset arrangement. Note the institution or issuer, country, account number, ownership percentage, maximum value, year-end value, and income connected to the asset. Do not let a local “savings,” “pension,” or “insurance” label decide the U.S. classification.

Next, separate the Form 8938 threshold calculation from the reporting analysis. Identify filing status and residence, document the applicable threshold for the filing year, and preserve the valuation method. Compare the Form 8938 inventory to the FBAR, Forms 3520, 5471, 8621, 8865, and the income-tax return. A duplicative-reporting rule may change what is entered on one form, but it does not erase the underlying asset, income, or workpaper.

Use [the fatca guide](/guides/fatca-guide) as the companion overview and [the fatca compliance service](/services/fatca-compliance) when the asset list contains an entity, trust, pension, insurance contract, or other item whose classification cannot be established from a statement alone.


## A practical evidence packet

The strongest way to use this page is to turn the question into a dated evidence packet rather than relying on a product label, a remembered balance, or a single tax-software screen. Start with the document that proves the legal relationship, then preserve the records that show what happened during the year. Keep the original file, the translated or summarized version if needed, and a short note explaining why the document belongs in the analysis.

Before deciding that a rule applies, record the filing year, your U.S. status, where you lived and worked, the legal owner of the asset or income, and any country-specific tax already paid. Those facts often change the result more than the name of the account or visa. Compare the result against [the fatca guide](/guides/fatca-guide), then use [the fatca compliance service](/services/fatca-compliance) when the inventory needs a documented review.

Use a simple decision log with four columns: fact, source document, rule or form affected, and unresolved question. Mark assumptions separately from verified facts. This makes it easier to update the work when an institution corrects a statement, a treaty protocol changes, or the IRS and FinCEN publish new instructions. It also prevents the same account or income item from being counted twice or omitted from a related form.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.

## Before submitting anything

Reconcile the draft against the source documents, prior-year filings, related information returns, and the final conversion or calculation worksheet. Confirm that names, identification numbers, dates, ownership percentages, currencies, and addresses are consistent. Save the filed copy, acceptance or confirmation record, and the working papers together. If the result depends on a legal opinion, treaty position, willfulness determination, immigration status, or a specialist classification, stop at the boundary described above and escalate that question rather than presenting an educational checklist as advice.
