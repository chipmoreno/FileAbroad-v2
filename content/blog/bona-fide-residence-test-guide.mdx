The **Bona Fide Residence Test** is the less rigid but more subjective path to qualifying for the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE). Instead of counting days, you must prove to the IRS that you have genuinely established residence in a foreign country.

## What Is the Bona Fide Residence Test?

To pass the Bona Fide Residence Test, you must:

1. Be a **bona fide resident** of a foreign country.
2. For an **uninterrupted period** that includes an **entire tax year** (January 1 – December 31).

Unlike the Physical Presence Test:
- There is **no 330-day requirement.**
- You can spend **more than 35 days** in the US.
- The test is **subjective**, based on facts and circumstances.

## Key Factors the IRS Considers

The IRS evaluates the **totality of your circumstances.** No single factor guarantees qualification, but the more factors you have, the stronger your claim.

### Factor 1: Length and Nature of Your Stay

- **Longer stays are better.** Living abroad for multiple years strongly supports bona fide residence.
- **Purpose matters.** Moving abroad for employment, retirement, or family reasons supports residence. Extended tourism does not.

### Factor 2: Intent to Remain Abroad

- Do you intend to stay indefinitely, or do you have a fixed return date?
- **Supporting evidence:** Employment contracts without end dates, open-ended leases, statements to banks and employers about your foreign address.

### Factor 3: Permanent Home Abroad

- Do you own or rent a home in the foreign country?
- Is it furnished and maintained as a primary residence?
- **Supporting evidence:** Lease agreements, property deeds, utility bills, homeowner's insurance.

### Factor 4: Local Integration

- Do you participate in local social, cultural, and economic life?
- **Supporting evidence:** Local club memberships, volunteer work, language classes, community organization participation.

### Factor 5: Visa and Residency Status

- Do you have a visa or residence permit that allows long-term stay?
- **Strongest:** Permanent resident visa, work visa with multi-year validity, retiree visa.
- **Weakest:** Tourist visa, visa-free entry with no long-term permission.

### Factor 6: Foreign Tax Registration

- Are you registered with the foreign tax authority?
- Do you file tax returns in the foreign country?
- **Supporting evidence:** Foreign tax ID number, tax registration documents, foreign tax returns.

### Factor 7: US Ties and Tax Home

- US family, economic, and personal ties can be relevant to where your abode and tax home are located.
- There is no universal checklist requiring you to close accounts, change voter registration, or surrender a driver's license. Keep the facts accurate and consistent across tax, immigration, banking, and state-residency records.

## Bona Fide Residence vs. Domicile

**Bona fide residence** is not the same as **domicile.**

- **Bona fide residence:** Where you actually live and work. Can change frequently.
- **Domicile:** Your permanent legal home. Harder to change and affects state tax obligations.

Federal bona fide residence and state domicile are different questions. A person may have a foreign federal tax home while a state continues to analyze domicile or statutory residency under its own rules.

## When Bona Fide Residence Is Better Than Physical Presence

| Situation | Best Test |
|---|---|
| Frequent US travel (40+ days/year) | Bona Fide Residence |
| Long-term expat (3+ years abroad) | Bona Fide Residence |
| Retiree with strong local ties | Bona Fide Residence |
| Recent mover (less than a full year) | Physical Presence |
| Digital nomad moving between countries | Physical Presence |
| Strict day-counting is feasible | Physical Presence |

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Assuming a Visa Alone Is Enough

A visa helps, but it is not sufficient by itself. You need the full package: home, local ties, tax registration, and intent.

### Mistake 2: Keeping a US Home as "Primary"

If you maintain a US home and treat it as your primary residence while claiming foreign bona fide residence, the IRS may challenge your claim.

### Mistake 3: Not Documenting Local Ties

The IRS won't take your word for it. Keep leases, utility bills, club memberships, and employment contracts.

### Mistake 4: Treating Immigration Status as the Entire Test

Immigration status can matter, but the IRS test turns on the nature and intent of the stay and the full facts. Do not assume that a visa label alone proves or defeats bona fide residence; obtain immigration advice separately if status is uncertain.

## How to Document Bona Fide Residence

Create a file with:
- Copy of foreign visa or residence permit.
- Lease agreement or property deed.
- Utility bills in your name.
- Foreign tax registration or tax returns.
- Employment contract or retirement documentation.
- Local bank account statements.
- Club or organization memberships.
- Evidence of US tie severance (if applicable).
