# U.S. Exit Tax for Green Card Holders and Long-Term Residents

Green card holders and other long-term residents can face U.S. expatriation tax when they terminate U.S. residency; U.S. citizens who relinquish citizenship can face the same statutory regime. “Exit tax” is a shorthand for the expatriation tax rules under sections 877A and 6039G. The analysis is not a single calculator and should begin before the termination or expatriation date.

## The covered-expatriate screening

The current Form 8854 instructions describe a covered-expatriate analysis that can involve:

- Net worth on the expatriation date.
- Average annual net income tax liability for the five tax years before expatriation.
- Certification that the person complied with federal tax obligations for those five years.

Dual citizens from birth, certain minors, and other taxpayers can have exceptions or special rules. An exception is not automatic; the person may still need to file Form 8854 and complete the certification requirement.

## What the mark-to-market regime means

If a covered expatriate is subject to section 877A, the rules generally treat property as sold for fair market value on the day before expatriation, subject to the statutory framework and exclusions. Deferred compensation, tax-deferred accounts, and nongrantor-trust interests can have separate treatment.

The date, asset type, basis, valuation, ownership, and tax characterization matter. A portfolio statement alone may not establish the correct basis or whether an exception applies.

## Form 8854 is more than a balance sheet

The form can require five-year tax information, balance-sheet data, expatriation facts, deferred-compensation details, and annual reporting after expatriation in certain cases. A person considering renunciation should collect prior returns, amended returns, information returns, asset basis, retirement statements, trust documents, and citizenship or residency records early.

## Do not plan from a single threshold

The average-tax-liability amount, exclusion amount, and other figures can be inflation-adjusted and change by tax year. The current Form 8854 instructions and applicable law control. Do not use a 2025 number for a later expatriation date without checking the current revision.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.
