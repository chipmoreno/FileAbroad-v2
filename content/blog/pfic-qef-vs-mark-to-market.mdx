# PFIC Mark-to-Market vs. QEF: Which Election Should You Make?

If you hold a PFIC, the applicable tax result depends on the stock, election history, available information, and the rules for QEF, mark-to-market, or a section 1291 fund. The comparison below is a framework for organizing the review, not a default election recommendation.

## QEF Election

### How It Works
1. Make the election under the applicable Form 8621 timing rules. The first holding year is important, but the IRS instructions include limited retroactive QEF procedures and deemed-sale or deemed-dividend rules in some cases.
2. Each year, report your pro-rata share of:
   - **Ordinary earnings** (taxed as ordinary income).
   - **Net capital gains** (taxed as long-term capital gains).
3. When you sell the PFIC, recognize capital gain or loss.

### Advantages
- The shareholder generally includes ordinary earnings as ordinary income and net capital gain as long-term capital gain under the QEF rules.
- A proper election changes the way later inclusions and distributions are handled, but the section 1291 rules can still matter for an unpedigreed or late-entered QEF.
- Losses, basis adjustments, and distributions follow the applicable QEF rules; they are not summarized by a single capital-gains shortcut.

### Requirements
- The PFIC must provide an **Annual Information Statement**.
- A fund must provide the required statement or an eligible intermediary statement; availability varies by fund.

## Mark-to-Market

### How It Works
1. Make the election under the applicable Form 8621 timing rules.
2. Each year, compare year-end FMV to adjusted basis.
3. Report the difference as **ordinary income** (if gain) or **ordinary loss** (if loss, limited to prior MTM gains).

### Advantages
- **No QEF Annual Information Statement is required for the MTM election itself.**
- The stock must be marketable under the IRS rules.
- The calculation can be simpler than QEF in some cases, but the first-election and section 1291 coordination rules still matter.

### Disadvantages
- Gains are **ordinary income** (up to 37%), not capital gains.
- **Losses are limited** — cannot exceed previously recognized MTM gains.
- No loss carryback; excess losses carry forward.

## Comparison Table

| Factor | QEF | Mark-to-Market | Excess Distribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual Info Statement required | Yes | No | N/A |
| Tax treatment | Ordinary earnings and net capital gain under QEF rules | Annual gain generally ordinary income; losses limited by prior MTM gains | Section 1291 allocation, tax, and interest rules may apply |
| Information needed | PFIC Annual Information Statement | Marketable-stock and year-end value data | Holding-period and distribution history |
| Loss treatment | Capital losses | Limited ordinary losses | No loss recognition |
| Complexity | High | Medium | Low (but terrible outcome) |

## Decision Framework

1. **Does the fund provide an Annual Information Statement?**
   - Yes → QEF may be available if the other requirements are met.
   - No → assess marketability, MTM eligibility, and the section 1291 consequences; do not choose by shortcut.

2. **Is this a long-term hold with expected significant appreciation?**
   - Yes → model the annual inclusion and eventual disposition under each permitted method.
   - Short-term or modest gains → MTM simplicity may be useful, but eligibility and prior-year rules still control.

3. **Are you comfortable with annual tax complexity?**
   - Yes → QEF.
   - Prefer simplicity → MTM.

## The Purging Strategy

If you have held a PFIC for years without an election, the IRS instructions describe possible deemed-sale, deemed-dividend, and retroactive-election procedures in limited circumstances. A sale and repurchase is not the only possible cleansing approach, and it can create U.S., foreign-country, and wash-sale consequences. Model the actual holding before taking action.
