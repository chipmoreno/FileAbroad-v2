# FATCA Foreign Asset Reporting for Americans Abroad

FATCA is often described as a foreign-bank reporting law, but an American living abroad may have a wider Form 8938 review. The form asks about specified foreign financial assets, the way those assets are held, their values, and related income. The asset inventory should therefore begin with ownership and legal structure—not only with a list of bank accounts.

- Foreign deposit, savings, and brokerage accounts.
- Foreign stock or securities held outside a financial account.
- Interests in foreign corporations, partnerships, trusts, or estates.
- Foreign-issued insurance or annuity products with investment value.
- Certain foreign pension and deferred-compensation interests.
- Financial instruments or contracts with a foreign issuer or counterparty.

The product name used by a local bank is not enough to classify the asset. Ask who legally owns it, who controls it, what institution holds it, and what income or distributions it produced.

## Form 8938 is not the FBAR

The FBAR is filed electronically with FinCEN and generally focuses on foreign financial accounts. Form 8938 is attached to the federal income-tax return and can reach specified foreign financial assets that are not ordinary accounts. Thresholds and exceptions differ.

The same account may be reported on both forms. Conversely, an asset can be relevant to Form 8938 without being an FBAR account, or relevant to another information return instead. A complete review compares the inventory against each form’s instructions.

## Value and currency evidence

Form 8938 uses fair-market-value concepts and U.S.-dollar reporting. Preserve statements for year-end and high-value periods, the exchange-rate source, ownership percentages, and the method used when values were unavailable. If a foreign pension or entity reports only in local currency, keep the source documents and the calculation behind the converted value.

Do not assume that the account’s current value is the maximum value for the year. Do not assume that a tax-sheltered label removes the asset from the analysis. The form’s rules, the filing status, and the legal structure control.

## Income and duplicative reporting

The asset may have generated interest, dividends, capital gains, rental income, pension income, or business income. Those amounts still need to be considered on the income-tax return even when the asset is also disclosed on an information form.

Form 8938 has rules for assets reported on certain other forms. A duplicative-reporting exception generally changes what is entered on Form 8938; it does not make the underlying asset invisible for every purpose. Record the other form and the relevant exception rather than simply omitting the asset from your working papers.

## A practical FATCA review sequence

1. Confirm whether an income-tax return is required and identify filing status.
2. Determine the applicable residence and threshold rules for the filing year.
3. List every potentially specified foreign financial asset by legal owner.
4. Obtain values, currency records, issuer details, and ownership documents.
5. Identify income and other U.S. information returns connected to each asset.
6. Complete Form 8938 only after the inventory and duplicative-reporting review are reconciled.
