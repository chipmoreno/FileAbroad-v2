# Form 3520-A for a Foreign Trust: Annual U.S. Owner Reporting

Form 3520-A is the annual information return for a foreign trust with a U.S. owner. It is not a substitute for Form 3520, and it is not simply a foreign bank statement. The filing identifies the trust, its owners and beneficiaries, and the information needed to report trust activity under the U.S. rules.

## Determine whether the arrangement is a foreign trust

U.S. tax classification uses a court test and a control test. A trust can be foreign for U.S. purposes even if local law calls it a pension, family settlement, savings plan, or insurance wrapper. Collect the trust deed, amendments, governing law, trustee information, beneficiary terms, and control rights.

## Identify the U.S. owner

The U.S. owner is determined under the grantor-trust rules, not merely by the name on a bank statement. Contributions, transfers, retained powers, beneficiary rights, and related-party arrangements can affect the analysis. Build an ownership timeline for the full year.

## When the trust does not file

The IRS instructions describe a substitute Form 3520-A process for a U.S. owner when the foreign trust fails to file. The substitute filing is attached to Form 3520 and requires the information and statements specified by the current instructions. Do not assume that a missing trustee report means no filing is required.

## Coordinate the annual forms

Form 3520 can report ownership, transfers, distributions, loans, uncompensated use, and foreign gifts or bequests. Form 3520-A provides the trust’s annual information. Form 8938 may also require disclosure, and a distribution may have income consequences. Reconcile the forms to the trust’s books, bank records, and beneficiary statements.

## Document checklist

- Trust instrument and amendments.
- Trustee and protector details.
- Contributions, distributions, loans, and use of trust property.
- Annual financial statements and beneficiary statements.
- U.S. owner and beneficiary information.
- U.S. agent appointment or required substitute documents.
- Related Form 3520, Form 8938, and income-reporting workpapers.

## Start with the governing documents

For a foreign-trust question, obtain the trust deed, amendments, governing law, trustee and protector details, beneficiary rights, contribution history, distribution statements, loans, and year-end accounts. Identify the grantor, owner, beneficiaries, and persons with control. Record each transfer into or out of the arrangement and connect it to the bank or investment statement that proves the amount and date.

Only then map the facts to Forms 3520, 3520-A, 8938, FBAR, and the income-tax return. Separate a foreign gift or bequest from a trust transfer, and separate a distribution from a loan or reimbursement. Check whether the arrangement is treated as a grantor or non-grantor trust for the relevant U.S. purpose and whether a U.S. owner creates an annual foreign-trust filing. The local name of a foundation, pension, savings plan, or family arrangement does not answer those questions.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.
