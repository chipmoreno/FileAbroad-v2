# How to Prepare a Streamlined Non-Willful Statement

Form 14653 is not a cover letter. It is a certification that the failure to report income, pay tax, file returns, and submit required international information returns was non-willful. The narrative should be complete, fact-specific, and consistent with the amended returns, FBARs, and supporting records.

## Explain the timeline

Start with a clear chronology:

- When you became a U.S. person or moved abroad.
- Where you lived and worked during each relevant year.
- What income, accounts, entities, or pensions existed.
- What you believed about U.S. filing obligations.
- When you learned about the obligations.
- What steps you took after learning about them.

Avoid vague phrases such as “I forgot” when the facts are more specific. Explain the source of the misunderstanding, the information available at the time, and why the omission occurred.

## Address every type of omission

The narrative should match the actual filing package. If you omitted Forms 1040, FBARs, Form 8938, Form 3520, Form 5471, Form 8621, or foreign income, identify the issue and explain it. A statement that discusses only the tax return while ignoring an omitted account can undermine the certification.

## Use records, not storytelling

Gather passport and residence records, employment documents, bank statements, prior advice, correspondence, tax notices, and the information you received from financial institutions. The explanation should be supported by the facts and should not claim reliance on advice that did not exist.

If a previous preparer gave incorrect advice, identify what was communicated and when. If no advice was obtained, say so. A concise truthful statement is stronger than a dramatic but unsupported narrative.

## Review the certification language

Read every representation in the current Form 14653 and its instructions. The scope of the certification is broader than “I did not know about FBAR.” Confirm that the amended returns, payment, FBAR filings, and international forms match the statement.

If the facts include deliberate concealment, false documents, transfers designed to avoid reporting, or a conscious decision not to learn the rules, stop and obtain attorney advice. Do not use Streamlined as a shortcut around a willfulness analysis.

## Final consistency check

Before submission, compare:

1. The years and residence dates in Form 14653.
2. The tax years and schedules in the amended returns.
3. The FBAR years and account inventory.
4. Forms 8938, 3520, 5471, 8621, or other information returns.
5. Tax, interest, payment, and delivery evidence.

## Turn the topic into a repeatable review workflow

Start with a complete fact inventory: filing year, U.S. status, residence and work locations, legal owner, dates, currencies, related entities, and prior filings. Gather primary documents before relying on summaries. Use the current agency instructions for the form or rule, preserve the source URL and access date, and write down any assumption that could change the result.

Then trace each fact to the return, information form, deadline, or disclosure it may affect. Reconcile the same item across the federal return, foreign return, account statements, and prior-year workpapers. If the issue involves a local-law classification, treaty position, immigration status, willfulness, or a penalty, record it as an escalation question rather than presenting a general article as a conclusion.

## A practical evidence packet

The strongest way to use this page is to turn the question into a dated evidence packet rather than relying on a product label, a remembered balance, or a single tax-software screen. Start with the document that proves the legal relationship, then preserve the records that show what happened during the year. Keep the original file, the translated or summarized version if needed, and a short note explaining why the document belongs in the analysis.

Before deciding that a rule applies, record the filing year, your U.S. status, where you lived and worked, the legal owner of the asset or income, and any country-specific tax already paid. Those facts often change the result more than the name of the account or visa. Compare the result against [the streamlined filing guide](/guides/streamlined-filing-guide), then use [the streamlined filing service](/services/streamlined-filing) when the inventory needs a documented review.

Use a simple decision log with four columns: fact, source document, rule or form affected, and unresolved question. Mark assumptions separately from verified facts. This makes it easier to update the work when an institution corrects a statement, a treaty protocol changes, or the IRS and FinCEN publish new instructions. It also prevents the same account or income item from being counted twice or omitted from a related form.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.

## Before submitting anything

Reconcile the draft against the source documents, prior-year filings, related information returns, and the final conversion or calculation worksheet. Confirm that names, identification numbers, dates, ownership percentages, currencies, and addresses are consistent. Save the filed copy, acceptance or confirmation record, and the working papers together. If the result depends on a legal opinion, treaty position, willfulness determination, immigration status, or a specialist classification, stop at the boundary described above and escalate that question rather than presenting an educational checklist as advice.

## Build a fact-to-form matrix

Before drafting Form 14653, build a matrix that connects each material fact to the filing component it affects. The rows should include each tax year, residence period, employer or business, account, pension, entity, income category, foreign tax paid, U.S. tax paid, notice, and prior advice. The columns should identify the source document, missing record, affected Form 1040 schedule, FBAR year, international information form, and sentence in the non-willful statement that needs support.

This matrix prevents the statement from becoming a general story that does not match the returns. If a foreign bank account was omitted, the account must appear in the FBAR inventory and in any applicable Form 8938 analysis. If a foreign company existed, the ownership and entity forms must be reconciled. If a pension or fund was omitted, identify whether the omission was income reporting, account reporting, classification, or more than one issue. The statement should explain the facts without inventing a conclusion about eligibility.

## Explain what the taxpayer knew and when

Non-willfulness is a factual certification, not a phrase to paste into a form. Describe the taxpayer’s education, work, residence, language, access to advice, understanding of U.S. citizenship, interaction with local advisers, bank communications, and the event that caused the U.S. filing issue to come to light. Distinguish what the taxpayer believed from what the current law required. If the taxpayer received a warning or advice and then acted differently, include that fact for attorney review rather than hiding it.

A strong chronology is specific without being theatrical. It names the year, country, account or income, source of the misunderstanding, and corrective action. It does not claim that the taxpayer relied on a professional who was never consulted, or that every omission had the same cause when the records show several causes. Keep contemporaneous emails, bank letters, local returns, prior advice, and notices that support the chronology.

## Check the residence requirement independently

The Streamlined Foreign Offshore path has its own residence and filing requirements. Do not prove residence only by saying the taxpayer lived abroad. Use passports, travel history, leases, visas, employment records, utility bills, school records, local returns, and evidence of the foreign home. Check the relevant tax years and the current IRS instructions. A taxpayer who moved during the period or maintained substantial U.S. ties may need a more careful analysis.

Do not call a short vacation or an address used for mail a foreign residence. Conversely, do not discard a genuine foreign residence because the taxpayer visited family in the United States. Build the day count and home facts together. Record ambiguity as an issue to resolve and obtain attorney or credentialed review when the facts raise willfulness or representation concerns.

## Reconcile tax, interest, and payment records

The amended returns, FBARs, information returns, foreign tax workpapers, and Form 14653 should use the same names, addresses, years, account owners, currencies, and residence dates. Compare the tax shown on each amended return with payments, refunds, interest, withholding, and the amount due. Save evidence of payment and the delivery method. A certification that says tax was paid cannot be supported by a return package that omits the payment calculation.

Review whether a foreign tax credit, FEIE, pension election, or prior-year carryforward changes the amended return. Use the current forms and instructions for each year. Do not backfill a current-year threshold or form rule into an earlier filing without documenting the tax-year source. If a number is estimated, state the estimation method and what record would replace it.

## Handle high-risk forms and referral boundaries

Streamlined filings may include Forms 3520, 3520-A, 5471, 8621, 8938, 8858, 8865, 8833, or other forms. Each form can raise classification or legal questions that exceed a basic return checklist. A foreign trust, controlled corporation, PFIC, treaty position, deliberate concealment, examination, appeal, or voluntary disclosure should be identified before a preparation engagement is accepted.

## What to keep after filing

Save the signed Form 14653, amended returns, FBAR confirmations, information forms, source documents, payment records, correspondence, and delivery receipts in one controlled file. Keep a future-year checklist that asks whether any account, entity, pension, fund, gift, inheritance, state move, or notice changed. A catch-up filing closes a set of periods; it does not make future reporting optional.

The safest next step is a fact review with the filing years, residence history, missing forms, accounts, entities, and notices. ## Separate preparation from certification judgment

The taxpayer signs the certification and remains responsible for its truth. Preparation support can organize facts, calculate return items, prepare accepted forms, and identify missing records, but it cannot turn an uncertain intention question into a safe answer. If the taxpayer cannot explain why an account, income source, or form was omitted, pause the draft and identify the uncertainty. An attorney may need to assess exposure before any certification is signed.

Do not use a prior professional’s work as automatic proof that the taxpayer acted reasonably. Review what the adviser was told, what the engagement covered, what advice was given, and whether the taxpayer followed it. Likewise, do not blame a bank letter for every omission if the taxpayer had already received a clear U.S. filing instruction. The statement should be accurate, supported, and proportionate to the records.
