# GILTI for Expat Business Owners: What to Review Before Filing

U.S. citizens and resident aliens who own or control a foreign corporation can face GILTI reporting even when the company keeps its profits abroad. GILTI is not a label for every foreign business and is not a simple tax on a bank balance. It is a calculation that starts with the CFC and ends on the U.S. shareholder’s return.

## Start with the CFC determination

Before calculating GILTI, identify whether the foreign corporation is a controlled foreign corporation under the current U.S. ownership rules. Review direct, indirect, and constructive ownership, voting power, value, related persons, and ownership changes during the corporation’s year.

The entity’s local-country classification does not answer the U.S. question. A company called a limited company, sociedad, corporation, or partnership may receive a different U.S. tax classification through an election or default rule.

## How Form 8992 fits with Form 5471

The IRS describes Form 8992 as the U.S. shareholder calculation of GILTI. Schedule A reports CFC-level information used in the calculation. Form 5471 can require separate schedules for the foreign corporation, including income, taxes, earnings, ownership, and related-party transactions.

The information flow often looks like:

1. Build the foreign corporation’s trial balance and tax adjustments.
2. Identify tested income, tested loss, excluded items, and related-party amounts.
3. Determine the shareholder’s pro rata share and ownership period.
4. Calculate the GILTI amount under the applicable-year rules.
5. Analyze foreign taxes and any available credit or deduction treatment.
6. Complete Form 8992, Form 5471, and other required forms consistently.

## Why “the company paid tax locally” is not enough

Foreign tax can be relevant but does not automatically make the U.S. calculation zero. The type of tax, the year paid or accrued, the CFC’s income category, the shareholder’s status, and the credit limitations matter. A local financial statement may not separate the items required for U.S. reporting.

## Records to collect

Collect the articles and ownership register, financial statements, trial balance, foreign return, tax payments, accounting policies, functional currency, intercompany agreements, distributions, shareholder loans, and prior Forms 5471 and 8992. Keep a schedule showing every change in ownership and the corporation’s accounting period.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.
