# How to Identify PFICs in Your Portfolio: A Checklist for US Expats

The PFIC rules are technical, and an early screening review can help identify questions before a foreign fund enters your portfolio.

## The Practical Rule

> If it is a foreign-domiciled pooled investment vehicle, treat it as a high-priority PFIC screening item and verify the issuer, structure, tests, and ownership chain.

This includes:
- Foreign mutual funds
- Foreign ETFs
- Foreign unit trusts
- Foreign investment trusts
- Foreign hedge funds
- Foreign private equity funds
- Foreign venture capital funds
- Foreign insurance wrappers with investment components

## The Red Flag Checklist

Before buying any foreign investment, check these red flags:

### Red Flag 1: Domicile Outside the US
- Is the fund registered in the UK, Ireland, Luxembourg, Germany, France, Switzerland, Japan, Australia, Canada, or any non-US jurisdiction?
- Is it listed on a non-US exchange?
- Does the prospectus state a non-US place of incorporation?

**If yes → PFIC review: HIGH PRIORITY**

### Red Flag 2: Pooled Investment Structure
- Does the fund pool money from multiple investors?
- Does the fund invest in stocks, bonds, or other securities?
- Is the fund managed by an investment manager?

**If yes → PFIC review: HIGH PRIORITY**

### Red Flag 3: Passive Income Orientation
- Does the fund primarily invest for dividends, interest, or capital appreciation?
- Does the fund description use words like "income," "growth," "balanced," or "index tracking"?

**If yes → PFIC review: HIGH PRIORITY**

### Red Flag 4: No Annual Information Statement
- Does the fund manager provide an Annual Information Statement for US taxpayers?
- Has the fund manager even heard of Form 8621 or QEF elections?

**If no → a QEF election may not be available for that holding. Mark-to-market may be available only if the stock is marketable under the IRS rules; otherwise the section 1291 rules may apply.**

## Screening Examples (Generally Not PFIC Stock by Themselves)

| Investment | PFIC Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| US-domiciled mutual funds and ETFs | Generally not PFICs themselves | Confirm legal domicile and structure |
| Individual foreign stocks | Usually lower screening risk | The issuer can still need a PFIC analysis |
| Foreign bank deposits and CDs | Generally not PFIC stock | May trigger FBAR/Form 8938 |
| Direct foreign real estate | Generally not PFIC stock | Structures and other reporting can change the analysis |
| US Treasury bonds | None | |
| Foreign government bonds (direct) | Low | Direct holding, not fund |
| Foreign currency | None | |
| Precious metals (physical) | None | ETFs are different story |

## What to Do If You Already Own PFICs

If you discover PFICs in your portfolio:

1. **Determine when you bought them.** QEF and mark-to-market elections have timing rules, but the IRS instructions include limited retroactive QEF regimes and special rules when an election begins after the first holding year. Do not assume a late election is either available or impossible.
2. **Check if the fund provides an Annual Information Statement.** If yes, make a QEF election on your next return.
3. **If no Annual Information Statement is available, assess MTM.** Mark-to-market requires marketable stock under the IRS rules and has its own first-election and section 1291 coordination rules.
4. **If neither election fits, the section 1291 rules may apply.** Model the reporting and tax consequences before selling, transferring, or restructuring the holding.
5. **Consider restructuring.** Sell the foreign funds and buy US-domiciled alternatives. Be aware of host-country capital gains tax on the sale.

## The Questions to Ask Your Advisor

Before investing through a non-US advisor, ask:

1. **"Is this investment a PFIC?"**
   - If they say "What's a PFIC?" — do not invest.
   - If they say "No, it's fine" — ask how they know. Demand documentation.

2. **"Does the fund provide an Annual Information Statement?"**
   - If yes, a QEF election may be possible if the other requirements are met.
   - If no, assess marketability and the section 1291 rules; do not promise that one election is always better.

3. **"What is the U.S. tax and reporting treatment for a U.S. person?"**
   - Ask for the issuer's legal entity, domicile, and supporting documentation rather than relying on a marketing label.

4. **"Are there US-domiciled alternatives?"**
   - VTIAX instead of a UK-domiciled global equity fund.
   - VXUS instead of a European emerging markets ETF.

## Scope note

This is a screening checklist, not a PFIC classification, election recommendation, or investment recommendation. The issuer's legal entity, income and asset tests, ownership chain, marketability, election history, account structure, and tax year must be reviewed before deciding whether Form 8621 or another filing applies.
