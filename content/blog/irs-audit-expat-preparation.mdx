An IRS examination is a formal process, but the first step is usually more practical than dramatic: understand exactly what the IRS is asking for and organize a response that addresses those issues only. Americans abroad often need to gather records from more than one country, convert amounts into U.S. dollars, and explain how Forms 2555, 1116, 8938, 3520, 5471, or FinCEN filings connect to the return.

The IRS publishes [audit process information](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/irs-audits) and explains that a notice or letter should identify the reason for contact and the next action. The notice controls the response channel, deadline, and requested documents.

## Build an issue list from the notice

Before uploading or mailing records, create a one-page issue list:

- tax year and return type;
- notice or examination reference number;
- each line, schedule, form, or transaction questioned;
- the IRS position as described in the letter;
- your position and the record that supports it;
- response deadline and delivery method;
- person authorized to communicate with the IRS.

This prevents an informal document dump. It also helps identify whether the IRS is asking about income, deductions, foreign tax credits, residency, account reporting, business activity, or a separate information return.

## Expat records to organize

Depending on the issue, assemble:

1. The filed federal return and every attached schedule or information form.
2. W-2s, 1099s, brokerage statements, pension statements, and foreign payer records.
3. Travel calendars, passports, leases, visas, and other records relevant to the FEIE or tax home.
4. Foreign tax returns, assessments, payment receipts, and exchange-rate methodology for Form 1116.
5. Foreign account statements showing year-end and maximum values for FBAR and Form 8938 analysis.
6. Entity, trust, pension, or fund documents when ownership or classification is questioned.
7. Copies of previous IRS correspondence and proof of timely responses.

Keep a copy of everything submitted. Redact unnecessary sensitive information from working copies, but do not alter records or discard originals. Organize files by tax year and issue rather than by the country where the document was issued.

## Foreign tax credit and FEIE questions

If the examination concerns the FEIE, document the days and foreign tax home that supported the original position. If it concerns the Foreign Tax Credit, reconcile the foreign income, foreign tax paid or accrued, source category, carryovers, and Form 1116 limitation. A change to foreign income can affect multiple forms, so recalculate connected schedules rather than answering one line in isolation.

## When specialist or legal representation is needed

Consider a qualified representative when the notice involves large adjustments, repeated years, foreign entities or trusts, potential penalties, a willfulness question, an appeal, or a request that may implicate privilege. FileAbroad can screen an accepted tax-preparation scope and coordinate records within that scope; legal representation and matters outside preparation authority should be referred to appropriate counsel.

For an initial scope review, [start the intake](/intake) with the tax year, notice type, response date, and issues listed by the IRS. Do not send Social Security numbers, account numbers, passports, or tax documents through an unprotected preliminary message.

## Turn the topic into a repeatable review workflow

Start with a complete fact inventory: filing year, U.S. status, residence and work locations, legal owner, dates, currencies, related entities, and prior filings. Gather primary documents before relying on summaries. Use the current agency instructions for the form or rule, preserve the source URL and access date, and write down any assumption that could change the result.

Then trace each fact to the return, information form, deadline, or disclosure it may affect. Reconcile the same item across the federal return, foreign return, account statements, and prior-year workpapers. If the issue involves a local-law classification, treaty position, immigration status, willfulness, or a penalty, record it as an escalation question rather than presenting a general article as a conclusion.

## A practical evidence packet

The strongest way to use this page is to turn the question into a dated evidence packet rather than relying on a product label, a remembered balance, or a single tax-software screen. Start with the document that proves the legal relationship, then preserve the records that show what happened during the year. Keep the original file, the translated or summarized version if needed, and a short note explaining why the document belongs in the analysis.

Before deciding that a rule applies, record the filing year, your U.S. status, where you lived and worked, the legal owner of the asset or income, and any country-specific tax already paid. Those facts often change the result more than the name of the account or visa. Compare the result against [the expat tax guide](/guides/expat-tax-guide), then use [the expat tax filing service](/services/expat-tax-filing) when the inventory needs a documented review.

Use a simple decision log with four columns: fact, source document, rule or form affected, and unresolved question. Mark assumptions separately from verified facts. This makes it easier to update the work when an institution corrects a statement, a treaty protocol changes, or the IRS and FinCEN publish new instructions. It also prevents the same account or income item from being counted twice or omitted from a related form.

## Common failure modes

The most common failure is treating a general answer as a conclusion for a specific structure. A foreign label, a tax-free local result, a treaty headline, or an old filing can be a useful lead, but none is a substitute for the current form instructions and the governing documents. Another failure is collecting only year-end numbers. Many cross-border tests depend on a maximum value, a transaction during the year, a residence date, or an election that must be preserved in the return workpapers.

Do not delete ambiguous records. Keep them in a review queue with the question they create. A good queue distinguishes “document missing,” “classification uncertain,” “value needs reconstruction,” and “professional interpretation required.” That separation lets straightforward work continue while protecting the issues that should not be guessed.

## Before submitting anything

Reconcile the draft against the source documents, prior-year filings, related information returns, and the final conversion or calculation worksheet. Confirm that names, identification numbers, dates, ownership percentages, currencies, and addresses are consistent. Save the filed copy, acceptance or confirmation record, and the working papers together. If the result depends on a legal opinion, treaty position, willfulness determination, immigration status, or a specialist classification, stop at the boundary described above and escalate that question rather than presenting an educational checklist as advice.
